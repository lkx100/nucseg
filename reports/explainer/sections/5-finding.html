Part 5

Where the missing points are

So far we looked at one number per run. This part opens that number up into the 603 photo scores behind it. All numbers here come from our best result, the average of four models.

  1. Each square is one photo, placed by its score. There are 603 of them, the worst on the left and the best on the right.
  2. The photo in the middle, the median, scores 0.938. Half of all photos score higher. The target is 0.937. So the typical photo is already there.
  3. But a run is graded by its average, and the average is 0.9235. A few very low scores pull it down, the way one zero pulls down a class average.
  4. 47 photos score below 0.85. That is 8% of the photos. Together they cost 1.46 points, more than the whole 1.35-point gap to the target.
  5. This is not bad luck on the day. When we average only two models instead of four, 44 of the same 47 photos are below 0.85 again. The model fails on particular photos.
  6. Three squares sit far to the left, at 0.00, 0.01 and 0.36. The next picture is about them.

One wrong answer key

We did not save the model's drawings for these photos. We know their size from the error counts: on photo A the wrong pixels cover 19% of the photo, and on photo B they cover as much as its whole answer key.

Two photos out of 49 falling to zero would cost about 3.8 points, so the size fits. This link is my explanation, not something we measured. It would also explain why fold 1 stopped early in all three runs of experiment 02.

  1. Among the 603 photos we score, exactly three have this size and look. They are 347 pixels wide and 260 high, and show the same kind of bright nuclei on a dark background.
  2. Here are their answer keys. Two are fine. The key of photo A is almost empty: 26 marked pixels on a photo full of nuclei. This answer key is wrong.
  3. Fold 0's turn. Photo A is in the exam. The model learned from photos B and C, so it marks the nuclei. The empty key says there are none. Score: 0.000.
  4. Fold 4's turn. Now photos B and C are in the exam and photo A is in the training set. It is the only photo of this look the model studies, and its key says: nothing here.
  5. So the model marks almost nothing on photo B and only a small part of the nuclei on photo C. Scores: 0.008 and 0.364.
  6. These three photos cost 0.40 points of the 1.35 we are missing. Without them the average would be 0.9275.
  7. The same wrong key explains an old puzzle. Photos B and C also sit in the stop set of fold 1. In that round the model trains on the empty key. The chart shows our baseline run C*: fold 1's stop-set score settles about 3 points under the other folds.

The rest of the low group

These are the 24 lowest-scoring photos, the three from above included. Hover over one, or tap it, to see its answer key. Many are very dark, so there is a switch to brighten them.

Of the 47 low photos, 32 are small fluorescence photos, 9 are stained tissue, 5 are large fluorescence photos and 1 is brightfield. Several are dark or out of focus. We have not yet looked at what the model drew on them, so we do not know why they fail.

The gap is not spread thinly over all photos. It sits in a small group. One cause is a single wrong answer key, and it reaches three photos and one stop set.
