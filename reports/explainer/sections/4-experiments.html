Part 4

Eight experiments, one win

Each experiment changes one thing and asks whether the score moves by more than luck. Step through them in the order we ran them.

  1. Experiment 01, run A. We start with the plain Swin-T U-Net from part 2, without the yellow branch. It scores 0.9178. This is the baseline.
  2. Run B: adversarial training. This is the idea behind NSL (neural structured learning). For every photo the model also trains on a copy with a tiny change, invisible to the eye, chosen to fool the model as much as possible. Score 0.9144, lower than the baseline on all 5 folds. It did make the model hard to fool: under such an attack its score stays at 0.918, where the baseline drops to 0.800. Training took 2.4 times as long.
  3. Experiment 02 first reran the baseline with nothing changed: 0.9143. The 0.35-point gap to the first run is our measure of luck, drawn as the shaded band.
  4. Run C: the full-size branch. This is the yellow branch from part 2. Score 0.9196, which is 0.53 points over the rerun and ahead on 4 of 5 folds. It passes the win rule. From here on, C is the model.
  5. Runs D and G: enlarged photos. We fed the model photos enlarged to twice the size, so each nucleus covers more grid cells. Without the yellow branch that gave 0.9190, with it 0.9196. No gain over C, at about 3 times the training time.
  6. Run E: adversarial training again, on top of C, with a change half as large. 0.9197. The model was again harder to fool (0.905 under attack against 0.818), and again the score did not move. We closed this idea for the Dice target.
  7. Run F: altered copies. The model trained on photos that were warped, recoloured, blurred and speckled with noise. 0.9213. That is less than 0.2 points over C, inside the luck band.
  8. Experiment 06: train longer. Training often stopped early, sometimes before epoch 30 of 60. We forbade stopping before epoch 30. C became C* at 0.9207, our baseline since then. F became F* at 0.9201. With training length equal, the altered copies added nothing.
  9. Experiment 07: average finished models. Nothing was trained. We averaged the answers of models we already had. Two models gave 0.9217. Four gave 0.9230. With the 8-orientation trick from part 3 that is 0.9235, our best score.
  10. Experiment 08: make edges count more. 83% of all wrong pixels lie within 2 pixels of a nucleus edge. So during training, mistakes near an edge counted 5 times as much. 0.9213. No gain.
  11. Step back. From run C on, eight single-model runs fit inside one luck band. Five different training ideas gave the same score. That tells us the limit is not in how we train. Part 5 looks at where it is.

One change to the model worked. Changes to the training did not. That is why the next part stops looking at runs and starts looking at single photos.
